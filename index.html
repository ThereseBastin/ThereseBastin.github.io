Working paper Wealth taxation

When Wealth Taxes Don’t Bite: Behavioural Responses to the Belgian Financial Wealth Tax

With Ilan Tojerow, Nikolaos Koutounidis, Milan van den Heuvel, and Constantine Yannelis

This paper examines behavioural responses to Belgium’s financial wealth tax using individual-level banking data. It studies whether taxpayers reduce taxable securities near the threshold and whether they adjust the composition of their portfolios after the reform.

Read abstract

We find no robust evidence that taxpayers reduced their total holdings of taxable securities to avoid the tax. Results are consistent across complementary bunching approaches. An event-study analysis nevertheless indicates changes in portfolio composition within the taxable base, including reallocation toward higher-return assets. The findings suggest that wealth taxes targeting measurable assets and relying on third-party reporting may limit avoidance responses, even when the taxable base is relatively narrow and liquid.

Work in progress Inheritance and gifts

Gift Taxation, Inter Vivos Transfers, and Beneficiaries’ Careers

With Ilan Tojerow and Malka Guillot

This project investigates how gift taxation affects the substitution between gifts and inheritances and how the receipt of intergenerational transfers shapes beneficiaries’ professional trajectories, with particular attention to gender differences.

Read project description

The project first studies whether taxpayers respond to changes in property-gift tax rates by changing the timing or form of intergenerational transfers. It then examines whether tax incentives and asset-specific exemptions reinforce gender disparities in transferred wealth. Finally, it analyses the effects of receiving gifts and inheritances on beneficiaries’ and spouses’ labour-market outcomes, exploiting a reform of property-gift taxation in Wallonia.
